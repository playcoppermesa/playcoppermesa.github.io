What is reining?

Reining is a western riding sport. A horse and rider perform a set pattern of moves in an arena, and judges score how precise, smooth and willing the horse is. It's sometimes called the dressage of western riding.

Every run starts at 70 points. Each move earns between −1½ and +1½.

Sliding stop

From a fast lope, the horse sits back on its hind legs and slides to a stop, leaving long tracks in the dirt.

Spin

The horse turns in place, pivoting on its inside back foot. Good spins are fast, flat and stop exactly on target.

Rollback

Right after a stop, the horse swings around over its hind legs and lopes off the other way in one smooth move.

Circles

Big fast circles and small slow ones show that the rider controls the horse's speed and shape.

Lead change

When the horse changes direction in a figure-eight, it switches its leading front leg mid-stride without breaking pace.

Back up

The horse backs up in a straight line, quickly and willingly, for a set distance.
